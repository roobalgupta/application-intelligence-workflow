# Master Application Intelligence Workflow

This file contains the core methodology of the Application Intelligence Workflow.

The candidate should not redesign this process for each application.

The professional context changes.

The workflow does not.

# Required Inputs

Before starting, obtain:

1. The completed candidate setup from `01_user_setup.md`
2. The candidate's Master CV
3. The complete job description for the role being assessed
4. The employer name
5. The job posting link where available

If important information is missing, identify what is missing rather than assuming it.

# Core Principle

Do not begin by rewriting the CV.

First determine whether the opportunity deserves the candidate's time.

Then understand what the employer is actually hiring for.

Then map the candidate's verified evidence against those requirements.

Only after that should the CV be tailored.

# Stage 1: Role Screening

Assess whether the role is genuinely worth pursuing.

Evaluate:

* Alignment with the candidate's target roles
* Alignment with the candidate's professional background
* Seniority
* Required years of experience
* Essential requirements
* Preferred requirements
* Industry relevance
* Transferability of experience
* Location
* Salary where stated
* Employment type
* Work authorisation requirements
* Sponsorship information where relevant
* Any explicit candidate non negotiables
* Any major practical hiring barriers

Produce two separate scores.

## Capability Fit

Score out of 10.

This assesses how closely the candidate's genuine professional experience matches the work required by the role.

Do not include practical hiring barriers in this score unless they directly affect the candidate's ability to perform the role.

## Realistic Shortlist Probability

Score out of 10.

This assesses how competitive the candidate is likely to be in the actual recruitment process.

Consider:

* Experience level
* Relevant evidence
* Seniority
* Industry familiarity
* Essential requirements
* Candidate competition
* Location
* Work authorisation
* Salary alignment
* Major missing capabilities
* Other practical hiring considerations

Capability and shortlist probability are not the same thing.

A candidate may be capable of performing a role while still facing practical barriers to being shortlisted.

# Role Screening Decision

Return one of the following:

Proceed

Borderline

Skip

Use the candidate's personal application rules from `01_user_setup.md`.

If no personal threshold has been provided, use 7 out of 10 Capability Fit as a general reference point rather than an automatic rule.

If the role clearly violates a non negotiable condition, recommend skipping even where professional fit is otherwise strong.

If recommending Skip, explain the reasons clearly and stop before CV tailoring unless the candidate explicitly asks to continue.

# Stage 2: Employer and Role Research

Where reliable current information and web access are available, research the organisation before tailoring the application.

Look for:

* What the organisation does
* Products or services
* Customers or users
* Business model
* Industry
* Current priorities
* Relevant growth or organisational changes
* Terminology used by the employer
* Relevant team information where publicly available
* UK presence where relevant
* Sponsorship information where relevant

Distinguish confirmed information from assumptions.

For UK sponsorship checks, do not assume that appearing on the Skilled Worker sponsor register means the employer will sponsor the specific vacancy.

Employer sponsorship eligibility and willingness to sponsor an individual role are separate questions.

If information cannot be verified, state that clearly.

# Stage 3: Job Description Analysis

Analyse the full job description before changing the CV.

Automatically extract:

1. Job title
2. Primary purpose of the role
3. Five to eight highest priority requirements
4. Essential requirements
5. Preferred or nice to have requirements
6. Required technical or functional capabilities
7. Repeated keywords and terminology
8. Seniority signals
9. Leadership expectations
10. Decision making expectations
11. Stakeholder expectations
12. Commercial or organisational expectations
13. Likely success measures
14. Required tools or platforms
15. Industry knowledge requirements
16. Any unusual or high risk requirements

Do not ask the candidate to manually determine these.

The system should determine them from the job description.

Do not treat every sentence in the job description as equally important.

Give greater weight to:

* Requirements repeated multiple times
* Requirements described as essential
* Responsibilities appearing prominently
* Capabilities connected directly to the purpose of the role
* Capabilities connected to measurable organisational outcomes
* Requirements clearly signalling the expected level of seniority

# Stage 4: Evidence Mapping

Before rewriting the CV, map the highest priority job requirements against verified candidate evidence.

For each important requirement identify:

* The requirement
* Candidate evidence
* Relevant employer, role or project
* Candidate's actual contribution
* Metric or concrete outcome where available
* Strength of match
* Any limitation
* Any missing evidence

Use the following match categories:

Strong

Moderate

Partial

Missing

Do not invent evidence simply because the job description asks for something.

If potentially relevant experience appears to exist but there is not enough information to use it safely, ask the candidate to confirm it.

Distinguish carefully between:

* Individual result
* Team result
* Department result
* Company result

Also distinguish between:

* Owned
* Led
* Managed
* Delivered
* Contributed
* Supported

Do not strengthen the wording beyond what the candidate's evidence can support.

# Stage 5: CV Tailoring

Only proceed to CV tailoring after completing Role Screening, Job Description Analysis and Evidence Mapping.

Apply the methodology in `03_cv_tailoring_rules.md`.

The tailored CV should prioritise verified evidence that corresponds to the employer's highest priority requirements.

Do not simply copy phrases from the job description.

Use employer terminology where it accurately reflects the candidate's genuine experience.

# Stage 6: Recruiter Reality Check

After producing the tailored CV, apply the full assessment in:

`04_recruiter_reality_check.md`

Do not inflate the assessment simply because the CV has already been tailored.

Evaluate the final document as if screening it against competing applicants.

# Stage 7: Gap Analysis and Factuality Review

Apply:

`05_gap_and_factuality_check.md`

Identify genuine weaknesses.

Identify presentation weaknesses.

Identify evidence that may exist but still needs candidate confirmation.

Check every major claim for factual support.

# Required Output Order

If the role should be skipped:

1. Capability Fit
2. Realistic Shortlist Probability
3. Skip rationale
4. Key gaps or barriers

Do not spend time tailoring the CV unless requested.

If the role should proceed:

1. Capability Fit
2. Realistic Shortlist Probability
3. Short role assessment
4. Complete tailored CV
5. Recruiter Reality Check
6. Ten Second Recruiter Test
7. Competitor Reality Check
8. Gap Analysis
9. Factuality Review
10. Final recommended improvements

# Final Principle

The goal is not to make every candidate appear suitable for every vacancy.

The goal is to identify where genuine fit exists and communicate that fit as strongly, clearly and credibly as possible.
