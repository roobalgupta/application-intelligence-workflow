# Gap Analysis and Factuality Check

This is the final quality control stage.

The purpose is to identify what is preventing the application from becoming stronger while ensuring that improvements remain truthful.

# Part 1: Gap Analysis

Identify every meaningful gap between the candidate's final CV and an exceptional application for the target vacancy.

For each gap provide:

## What is missing?

Describe the missing requirement, evidence or positioning.

## Why does it matter?

Explain its importance in the context of this specific vacancy.

## What type of gap is it?

Classify it as one of the following:

Genuine experience gap

Evidence gap

Presentation gap

Keyword gap

Seniority perception gap

Industry familiarity gap

Practical hiring barrier

## What could improve it?

Identify genuine existing evidence that might strengthen the application.

If the candidate may have relevant experience that has not yet been captured, explain what they should check or confirm.

## Suggested improvement

Provide a proposed CV change only where genuine supporting evidence exists.

Do not fabricate experience to close the gap.

# Part 2: Candidate Confirmation Questions

Where additional genuine experience could materially improve the application, ask specific questions.

Examples:

Did you personally own this activity or contribute to it?

Can you quantify the result?

How many users, customers, projects, transactions or stakeholders were involved?

Did you make the decision or execute somebody else's decision?

Which tools did you personally use?

Was this your individual result or a team result?

Did the outcome affect revenue, cost, efficiency, quality, adoption, risk or customer experience?

Did you manage people directly?

Did you lead stakeholders without formal management authority?

Can the result be verified or defended confidently in an interview?

Only incorporate new information after the candidate confirms it.

# Part 3: Factuality Review

Review every major claim in the tailored CV against the candidate's source information.

Check:

* Job titles
* Employers
* Employment dates
* Education
* Qualifications
* Certifications
* Metrics
* Budgets
* Revenue
* Team sizes
* Tools
* Platforms
* Technical capabilities
* Management responsibilities
* Geographic scope
* Industry claims
* Customer exposure
* Project ownership
* Commercial outcomes
* Research outcomes
* Portfolio projects

# Attribution Check

Make sure the CV distinguishes correctly between:

Owned

Led

Managed

Built

Delivered

Contributed

Supported

Individual result

Team result

Department result

Company result

Do not combine these in ways that exaggerate responsibility.

# Interview Defensibility Test

For every major claim, ask:

Could the candidate explain exactly what they did?

Could they explain how the result was measured?

Could they explain their personal contribution?

Could they explain the tools or methods used?

Could they answer follow up questions without contradicting the CV?

If not, revise the wording.

# Final Application Check

Before considering the CV ready, confirm:

* The first half of page one explains why the candidate fits the role
* The strongest evidence is visible
* The highest priority job requirements are addressed
* Important keywords appear naturally
* The CV demonstrates the appropriate level of seniority
* Claims are supported
* Metrics are credible
* Job titles remain accurate
* There is no invented experience
* There is no unnecessary repetition
* The document is concise
* The application can be defended confidently in an interview

# Final Output

Provide:

1. The most important remaining gaps
2. The improvements that can be made immediately
3. The questions requiring candidate confirmation
4. Any claims that should be softened or removed
5. The final recommended CV changes
